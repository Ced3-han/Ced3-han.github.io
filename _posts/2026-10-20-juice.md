![Figure 1 of the paper: the JUICE architecture, and text-to-image generation, visual understanding and joint generation of a maze route with its moves](/assets/images/covers/2026-juice.jpg)

Most unified multimodal models treat images and text differently. A pretrained language model writes the text and drives a diffusion head that draws the image, or images are quantized into discrete tokens, and vision is usually added to a model that was first pretrained on language. In our new paper we ask whether one process, trained on both modalities from scratch, can generate both.

**JUICE** (Joint Unified multimodal modeling In Continuous Encoder spaces) works directly in the continuous spaces of frozen pretrained encoders: SigLIP2 or WebSSL-DINO for images, Flan-T5 for text. A single flow matching Transformer, trained with no language model initialization, denoises both streams. Each modality has its own noise level, so text-to-image generation, captioning, visual question answering and joint generation are paths through one square of noise levels, all served by one set of weights.

Controlled pretraining runs settle the design. Generating text as continuous states keeps image generation level with a model trained only for text-to-image generation, at a cost in exact wording. Training has to cover both the edges of the square and its interior. Semantic encoder spaces are better targets than VAE latents or raw pixels. Scaled to 2.1B parameters at 224×224, the two models reach up to 0.88 on GenEval and 73.1 on VQAv2.

Because JUICE models the joint distribution, it can also draw a route through a maze and write the matching moves together. With coupled sampling the two outputs agree without a fixed generation order: joint success rises from 54.6% with independent sampling to 93.7% (SigLIP2 model adapted to mazes).
